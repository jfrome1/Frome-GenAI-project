Instructors often have difficulty setting GenAI course policies because we don't know how different GenAI policies might affect student learning. For classes that rely on out-of-class assessments, banning GenAI use is not a solution, as it is likely that some students will use GenAI regardless of course policy.

In the long term, this research project aims to understand howGenAI use affects student learning, so instructors can make evidence-based decisions about GenAI course policies. As a first step, we are trying to understand how students currently use GenAI in their coursework. We are inviting instructors to allow some student use of GenAI in their courses on the condition that the students submit links to their chats for potential review. Our view is that it is better to know how students are using GenAI, and looking at actual chats is a much more reliable way to gather use data than student self-report.

## FAQs

### Shouldn't we discourage GenAI rather than encouraging it?

While there is plenty of speculation, there is almost no research on the educational effects of GenAI use. We assume that some use will be harmful and some will be helpful. The first step is knowing how students actually use it.

Further, many of our students are using GenAI whether or not instructors allow it. It takes little effort to bypass GenAI detection. And even if we ban GenAI use, it's nearly impossible to prove its use or penalize students for using it.

### Won't students just say they didn't use GenAI rather than providing links to their chats? What's in it for them?

We have developed a class protocol that has lead to very high compliance in past courses. All students must either share a link or positively declare that they didn't use GenAI. Either option takes just 10 seconds, so if students do use GenAI, it takes the same effort to lie as to share their chat. In addition, 100% compliance isn't necessary for this project to be useful. Even if only 50% of students comply, you will still know a *lot* more about their GenAI use than if you don't request links.

### What GenAI policy would I have to set to participate?

You can set any GenAI policy you like. The only requirements are that you would allow at least some GenAI use in your course and that you ask students who do use GenAI to submit links to their GenAI chats. If requested, we are happy to help you develop GenAI policy options appropriate for your particular classes.

### What is the time commitment for participating in the project?

Participation requires little time commitment. You have your students submit GenAI chat links during the semester. After the course ends, we give students the option to opt-out of having their chats anonymized and analyzed for research, and you send me the remaining links that you've collected. If you are interested in participating at a higher level, we welcome Co-Investigators.

### If I'm interested in participating, what are the next steps?

- Contact me - frome(at)nus.edu.sg to get more information or ask questions
- If you decide to join, I'll offer to create a dedicated GenAI policy webpage for your course on [the student-facing project website](https://nus-ai-writing-project.vercel.app/) that you can distribute to your students  (optional, not required)
- At the end of the semester, you'll give your students a chance to opt-out of the study, and send me GenAI links for those who agree to participate

Feel free to invite instructors from other courses or institutions to join the project!
